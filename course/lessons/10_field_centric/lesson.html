Field-Centric Driving

Your robot drives — but "forward" on the stick means "the way the robot is pointing." Once the robot spins around, forward looks backward to the driver, and steering gets confusing fast.

Field-centric driving fixes that. "Forward" on the stick always means "away from the driver," no matter which way the robot faces. To do it, we rotate the driver's request by the robot's current heading before mixing the wheels.

Rotate, then mix

If the robot is turned by some heading, we spin the stick vector back by that same angle. Then the same mecanum mix from the last lesson does the rest — now relative to the field.

Open Drivebase.java and find the spot just before the mecanum mix, where forward and right are set from the raw sticks.

double cos = Math.cos(-headingRadians);
double sin = Math.sin(-headingRadians);
right   = strafe * cos - drive * sin;
forward = strafe * sin + drive * cos;

The headingRadians value is handed in every loop — it comes straight from the IMU you set up last lesson. Because the sensor is already reporting a real heading, this rotation takes effect the moment you add it: turn the robot and "forward" stays pointed away from you.

Hint

Remember the right bumper resets the heading to 0 — it re-zeros "forward" to whichever way the robot is currently facing. Press it at the start of a match with the robot pointed downfield.
